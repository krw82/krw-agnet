Treat the latest reported quarter (10-Q) as the main evidence source, with the
latest annual 10-K as the baseline. The system now retrieves both quarterly and
annual filings automatically. Map the current quarter, same quarter a year
earlier, and the latest annual filing baseline without mixing incompatible
periods, scopes, units, or accounting bases. Read management discussion and
explanatory notes before expanding missing metrics.

Build the analysis as a causal chain that connects five layers. Each layer's
numbers should be traced to their source document (quarterly 10-Q vs annual
10-K) and the chain should explain *why* a number moved, not just that it moved.

1. **Revenue composition**: Break total revenue into product/service segments
   when available (look for BusinessActivity evidence — iPhone Sales, Services
   Sales, etc.). Identify which segment drove growth and whether the mix is
   shifting toward higher- or lower-margin business.

2. **Geographic or segment growth drivers**: Identify which regions or segments
   grew fastest and what the filing says drove that growth. Quote the filing's
   own explanation (e.g., "price decreases", "product mix", "demand recovery").

3. **Cost trend**: Compare R&D and SG&A growth rates against revenue growth. If
   costs are growing faster than revenue, flag the margin pressure. If the
   filing explains the cost increase (e.g., "infrastructure-related costs"),
   quote it.

4. **Margin transmission**: Connect segment mix → gross margin → operating
   margin. Explain how the revenue mix shift (layer 1) flows through gross
   margin to operating margin, and where cost growth (layer 3) partially
   offsets the improvement. Use the chain: product/service mix → blended gross
   margin → operating expense burden → operating margin.

5. **Cash generation**: Connect operating income → working capital changes →
   operating cash flow → capital expenditure → free cash flow. State whether
   earnings growth translated into cash growth, and whether capital return
   (buybacks, dividends) is accelerating.

Reconcile commentary with representative reported numbers across all five
layers. Separate core operations from accounting effects, working-capital
timing, investment burden, acquisitions, financing, and capital allocation.
Classify the thesis effect as strengthened, intact, mixed, weakened, or
requiring re-underwriting based on the combined evidence from all five layers.
Do not infer consensus results, price reaction, or valuation inputs that were
not supplied as trusted run context.

# Wide research analysis

Use the covered-universe ontology to answer the user's market or sector question,
not to produce an unrequested stock screen. First establish the transmission
path: what changes, which business or industry objects receive the shock, and
which financial or operating outcomes could move. Then compare supporting and
contradicting evidence across the covered companies or cohorts.

Keep three layers separate:

1. directly observed ontology evidence and its period;
2. the causal interpretation that links the evidence;
3. scenarios or implications that remain uncertain.

When the question is broad, it is acceptable to split it into several related
objectives when each objective covers a distinct transmission path. Do not turn
every objective into a ticker recommendation. Use `query_universe` or
`trace_universe` only when it can change the conclusion, and stop when the
remaining gap is contextual rather than load-bearing. A thin or unavailable
universe must still produce a useful, explicitly qualified answer.

The final answer should lead with the conclusion, identify likely winners and
losers or exposed channels only when the evidence supports them, and end with
the next indicators worth monitoring. Never imply that a covered-universe
result is a complete market census.

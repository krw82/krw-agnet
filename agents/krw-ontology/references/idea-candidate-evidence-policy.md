# Candidate Evidence Policy

## Core Rule

Advance candidates from filing evidence, not from theme familiarity.

The first task is to translate the user's broad idea into a screen. Evidence is
judged against that screen, not against the company's general quality.

The strongest candidate evidence usually combines:

```text
recent management commentary
specific business activity
orders, backlog, contracts, pricing, volume, or demand
a metric or financial-statement connection
an identified burden or counter-signal
a reason this candidate deserves research priority now
```

## Evidence Priority

Prefer:

```text
1. Latest MD&A or management discussion explaining actual change
2. Financial statement notes and segment, backlog, contract, debt, capex, or cash-flow disclosures
3. Specific business-section evidence for products, customers, capacity, and market role
4. Specific risk language that confirms an already-visible operating or financial channel
```

Use generic risk factors only as supporting context.

## Candidate Promotion

An A candidate should answer all of these:

```text
What does the company actually do that connects to the screen?
What changed or remained important in the latest filing?
How could the driver reach a financial line item?
Why should this candidate be researched before other candidates in the screen?
What burden could offset the benefit?
What evidence would reject the idea first?
```

If one material answer is missing, use B or C.

Why-now evidence does not mean a stock catalyst. It means the latest filing made
the exposure, conversion path, burden, or rejection risk important enough to
prioritize.

## Numbers And Commentary

Numbers show what changed. Commentary and notes explain why it changed.

Use exact numbers when they:

```text
prove materiality
show a turning point
distinguish two candidates
change the priority bucket
```

Do not rank candidates by the number of extracted metrics or quotes.
Do not rank candidates by brand familiarity or common theme association.

## Cross-Company Consistency

Evaluate candidates on the same axes and comparable time context.

Do not:

```text
use latest-quarter evidence for one company and stale annual evidence for another without saying so
compare segment evidence with total-company metrics as if they were equivalent
reward a company merely because its filings contain more searchable text
```

## Unsupported Inputs

Without external data, do not claim:

```text
current valuation is cheap or expensive
the market has or has not priced in the idea
consensus estimates will rise
institutional ownership is crowded
short interest or borrow is favorable
the idea fits a portfolio or benchmark
```

Use filing-supported expectations risk instead:

```text
the operating case requires stronger conversion
the capital burden may delay cash-flow benefits
the latest filing does not yet prove material revenue contribution
```
